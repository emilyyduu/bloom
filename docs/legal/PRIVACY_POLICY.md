# Bloom Privacy Policy

Effective date: November 1, 2026

*Bloom is a student project. This policy was written by the developer and has not been reviewed by a lawyer.*

## Who we are
Bloom is made by Emily Du, based in Victoria, Australia.
Contact: eduu0005@student.monash.edu.

## The short version
- Bloom has no purchases and no ads.
- Bloom has no cookies. It is a mobile app, not a website.
- Bloom does not use analytics or advertising trackers and does not track you across other apps or websites.
- Bloom does not read your contacts. Your phone number, if you use one, stays with the sign in service and is not shown to other people.
- Your notes are stored on your device first and backed up to your account in our database. They are shared with friends only when you choose.
- You can delete your account, and everything we hold about it, inside the app.

## Minimum age
You must be at least 16 to use Bloom, and you confirm this when you sign up. We do not knowingly collect data from anyone younger. If you think a younger person has an account, contact us and we will delete it.

## What we collect and why

| What | Why | Where it is kept |
|---|---|---|
| Email address and password (if you sign up with email) | To sign you in | Firebase Authentication (Google). Your email is not copied into our database |
| Phone number (if you sign up with a phone) | To sign you in with a text code | Firebase Authentication only. It is not copied into our database and other people cannot see it |
| Display name and username | Shown to other Bloom users, and the username lets people search for you | Our database |
| Profile picture (optional) | Shown on your profile | Our database |
| Notes: text, dates, whether a note is a draft, shared or pinned | To run your journal, back it up and show it to friends you share with | Your device, and a backup in our database |
| Photos you attach to notes | Same as notes | Your device and our database (a small compressed copy) |
| Voice memos (up to 60 seconds) | Same as notes | Your device and our database |
| Friend requests and friendships | To connect you with friends | Our database |
| Blocked people | To hide them and stop their requests | Our database. Only you can see your list |
| Circles (private named groups of friends) | To choose whose plants you see. Only you can see them in the app | Our database |
| Streak (current count, last post date) | To grow your plant in the Shared Garden. Your friends can see it | Our database |
| Comments, replies and reactions | Social features on shared notes | Our database |
| Notification feed items (who did what, with a short preview) | To show your in-app notifications list | Our database |
| Reports you send about a note, comment or person | To keep Bloom safe | Our database. Only the developer can read them |

### Movie and show search
When you search the Library, your search words are sent to The Movie Database (TMDB) to get results, and posters are downloaded from TMDB. TMDB sees your IP address and the search words. Bloom does not send your name or account to TMDB. See TMDB's privacy policy: https://www.themoviedb.org/privacy-policy. This product uses the TMDB API but is not endorsed or certified by TMDB.

### Camera, microphone and notifications
These are optional. Bloom asks only when you use the feature that needs it. Photos you pick with the system photo picker are shared with Bloom only for the photos you choose. Bloom does not ask for access to your contacts or location.
Notifications are created by the app while it is running, and the daily reminder is scheduled on your device. Bloom does not send push notifications from a server. If that changes, we will update this policy first.

### Signing in
Phone sign in uses Google's Firebase Authentication, which sends the text message and may use device checks (for example a silent notification or a reCAPTCHA check) to stop abuse. Google may see technical details such as your IP address and device type when you sign in or use the app. Google explains what Firebase collects at https://firebase.google.com/support/privacy.

## Who can see your information
- **Only you:** your drafts, notes you have not shared, circles, blocked people, movie log, covers and settings.
- **Your accepted friends:** your display name, username, profile picture, streak and plant, and notes you mark as shared. The app shows friends the notes you shared today and your pinned notes. The database is a little looser than the app: an accepted friend who bypassed the app could read a note you shared in the last 30 hours, or any shared note you pinned. Older shared notes that are not pinned are not readable by friends.
- **Other Bloom users:** any signed in Bloom user can look you up by username and see your display name, username and profile picture. They can send you a friend request. If you block someone they can no longer send you requests.
- **Us:** the developer can access the database through the Firebase console. We will only do this to run, secure and fix the service, to review a report, or to act on a legal request.
- **Service providers:** Google (Firebase Authentication and Cloud Firestore) stores and processes account and backup data and sends sign in text messages and password reset emails. Google may process data in countries other than yours. TMDB receives search words as above.
- We do not sell your data and do not use it for advertising.

## Reports and blocking
You can report a note, a comment or a person, and block people. A report holds your account id, the id of the person or content, the reason, anything you add, and a copy of up to 500 characters of the text you saw. The developer reads reports to decide whether to remove content or close an account. The person reported is not told who reported them. We keep reports only for as long as we need them to deal with the report and keep Bloom safe, not for a fixed period. A report about another person or their content is kept after you delete your own account, because it is about someone else and may be needed to act on it.
Blocking removes any friendship between you and the other person, stops them sending you requests, and hides what they write. They are not told.

## Keeping and deleting your data
Your data stays until you delete it or delete your account. Deleting a note removes it from your device and from our database, along with its photo, voice memo and comments. Deleting a movie or show from your Library removes it from your device and from our database.
To delete your account, open Settings, then Delete account. You sign in again to confirm. Bloom then erases from our database your profile, username reservation, picture, notes, photos, voice memos, circles, blocked people, Library, notification feed, streak, friendships, your comments and reactions on friends' notes, and the notification items you sent to friends. It deletes your sign in, and it removes your data from your device. If a step cannot finish you stay signed in and can try again.
Limits: a copy may remain in Google's backups and logs for a limited time after you delete something. We don't control how long. Anything you posted that a friend has already seen, copied or screenshotted is outside our control. Reactions you made on friends' notes before November 1, 2026 may remain until the note is deleted.

## Your choices and rights
You can edit your profile, delete notes, stop sharing, remove or block friends, and turn off permissions in iOS Settings. You can ask us to access or correct your data at the email above. Contact us first and we will try to sort out any problem.
Depending on where you live you may have further rights. In Australia, the Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles apply to some organisations, and you can complain to the Office of the Australian Information Commissioner (https://www.oaic.gov.au). If you live somewhere else, such as the European Union or the United Kingdom, you may have other rights, for example to ask us to delete your data or give you a copy of it.

## Security
Data travels over encrypted connections and is stored in Google's cloud, protected by access rules that we write and test. It is not end to end encrypted, so we and Google's systems can technically access it. No system is perfectly secure.

## Changes
We will update this policy when the app changes and show the effective date above. For big changes we will tell you before they apply, for example by email.
